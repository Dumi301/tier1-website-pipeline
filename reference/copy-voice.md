# Copy Voice — reference

Page-level marketing and narrative copy only. ALL UX microcopy (buttons,
errors, forms, empty states) is a full defer to impeccable `clarify.md` — zero
restatement here.

## How to use

This file governs headline/body/section copy voice and specificity. It does
not touch button labels, error messages, form instructions, or any other
microcopy — those are impeccable `clarify.md`'s job entirely.

## Owner-voice register

Default for local-business sites: owner-voice, first-person-adjacent — not
third-person brochure copy. The fix direction: "trusted neighbourhood shop, the owner knows your car" —
bring back the personal/story feel.

| Generic (brochure) | Owner-voice (rewrite) |
|---|---|
| "We provide quality auto repair services." | "I've been under the hood of cars on this street for 12 years — bring yours in, I'll tell you straight what it needs." |
| "Our team is dedicated to customer satisfaction." | "You'll talk to me, not a call center — I answer the phone." |
| "State-of-the-art facility." | "Same lift I've had since 2014, kept in shape, does the job right." |
| "We take pride in every repair." | "If it's not right, I redo it before you pay for it." |
| "Serving the community for years." | "Corner of [street], same spot since [founding year] — ask anyone on the block." |
| "Contact us for a free quote." | "Drive by, I'll take a look and give you the number on the spot." |

## Specificity-from-research rule (binding)

Every section cites at least one concrete fact from the research brief — a
real street, a founding year, a named material/process/machine. Generic
claims ("quality service", "years of experience") are banned unless attached
to a real number or name.

## Headline formulas

- **Specific-claim headline** — states one real fact as the headline, not a
  mood. "Same lift since 2014" beats "Quality you can trust."
- **Question → owner-answer** — a real customer question, answered in the
  owner's voice directly beneath it.
- **Sensory-detail** — a concrete, specific sensory image drawn from the
  research brief (a smell, a sound, a material) rather than an abstraction.
- **Direct address** — speaks to "you" and names the actual situation the
  visitor is in, not a generic audience segment.
- **Banned mad-libs pattern:** `[Adjective] [Category] for [Audience]` — e.g.
  "Reliable Auto Repair for Busy Families." This is the templated-AI-copy
  tell; any headline matching this shape gets rewritten.

## Banned generic phrases

Blocklist — none of these ship without a real number or name attached:

"quality you can trust" · "your one-stop shop" · "experience the difference"
· "we take pride in" · "passion for excellence" · "customer satisfaction is
our priority" · "state-of-the-art" (unqualified) · "years of experience"
(unqualified) · "committed to excellence" · "second to none"

Also banned: en-dashes and em-dashes inside body copy — flagged as an AI tell. Use a period or a comma instead.

## Rhythm / sentence variety

Vary sentence length and structure across sections — no uniform cadence.
Three consecutive sections built on the same sentence shape (subject-verb-
object, same length) read as generated even when the words differ.
